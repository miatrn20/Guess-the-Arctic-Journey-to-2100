PROTOTYPE WRITEUP · DSC 106

Where we are now.

What have we done so far?

We picked our story: readers guess how much the Arctic will warm by 2100, then compare their guess with CanESM5 projections from CMIP6. We built the full page structure with five chapters: the hook, the guessing game, a polar "time machine," a regional comparison, and a "race to +3°C" chart. The guessing chart already works: readers can drag the point or use the slider, pick a scenario, and reveal the model's 2081–2100 Arctic warming. We also built a D3 dot plot that shows when each region first crosses +3°C, with animated transitions when the scenario changes and tooltips on hover. Our data pipeline (Kelvin to °C, 1995–2014 baseline, latitude-weighted means for the Arctic north of 66.5°N) comes from our Project 3 work.

What will be the most challenging part?

The hardest part will be the polar "time machine" map. We need to load gridded CMIP6 temperature data, project it onto a polar view in D3, and update the colors smoothly as the reader moves the year slider. The raw files are very large, so we must shrink them into small JSON or CSV files that still load quickly in the browser. Another challenge is turning the guess into a true "draw your own line" chart, where the reader sketches a full curve from 2020 to 2100 and we compare it year by year with the real data. Finally, we need to link all charts to one shared scenario and year, so the story stays clear and the charts do not feel disconnected.
